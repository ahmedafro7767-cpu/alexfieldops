Faults are dispatched by arrival, not by impact.

The Alexandria field team receives faults through a manual hand-off and splits them within the shift. Four gaps follow from that.

Order

A fault on a site that feeds many others can wait behind a minor one.

History

What was done on earlier visits is asked for by phone and chat, not read from the site.

Effort

Time on site and work done are not recorded in a form that adds up by site or area.

Knowledge

Access routes and site quirks leave with the engineer who knew them.

One place from incoming fault to closed visit.

See the whole queue at once

Faults sit with the field team that owns them, each carrying its importance score, fault type and the number of sites that depend on it. Unassigned faults can be distributed in one step, most important first.

  • Import faults pasted from a Remedy export
  • Tier and score on every fault
  • Map link from the site coordinates

Know what to do before dispatch

Each fault is classified and given an assessment: its impact, the upstream sites to check first, whether the site keeps coming back, and which other open faults share the hub. A checklist for the team follows from the fault type.

  • Rule-based analysis that works with no connection
  • Optional deeper analysis by AI, labelled as AI
  • Suggested team by load and area

Brief the team in one message

The dispatcher gets a ready message with the site, address, map link, checks and last visit. It can be edited, copied, or opened in WhatsApp or email. Nothing is sent automatically, and the platform records when the message was marked as sent.

Manage areas, sites and teams with numbers

The analytics view ranks areas by the attention they need, shows each team's workload and results, and lists the sites that needed two or more visits in 30 days so their root cause can be reviewed.

  • Area performance table
  • Fault types and weekly visits
  • Insights written from the live figures

A site's importance comes from its role in the network.

Scores run from 0 to 100 and combine facts from the site inventory with live fault and visit data. The weights are starting values, open to tuning with Vodafone and Ericsson engineers.

An add-on layer beside the systems already in use.

The platform starts read-only. It reads faults and site data, gives the dispatch desk and the shift manager one view, and writes nothing back until IT and security approve it.

The dashed line is a later step that needs a read-only account and the interface Ericsson approves. The site data comes from the Alexandria inventory workbook and the KMZ file.

Success is judged against a baseline taken before the pilot: time from fault to assignment, time to arrival, time on site, repeat visits and the share of visits with a complete record. Targets are agreed once the baseline exists.

Where the prototype stands

  • It runs on a sample of the Alexandria site inventory and the KMZ coordinates.
  • The visit records and the AI analysis texts shown in the demo are examples written for the demonstration.
  • It is not connected to Remedy or the Ericsson network management system. Faults are imported by paste.
  • Connecting it needs approval from Vodafone IT and security and the interfaces Ericsson provides.
